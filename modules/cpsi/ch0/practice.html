Counting (analyse combinatoire)

↑ contents
A1

A menu

Warm-up

A restaurant offers 3 starters, 4 main dishes and 2 desserts. A meal is one starter, one main and one dessert. How many different meals are possible?

Show solution

Three choices one after the other: multiplication rule (PFAC). 3 × 4 × 2 = 24 meals.

A2

Which formula?

Warm-up

For each situation, pick the counting formula (n objects available, p chosen).

1. A team of 3 people chosen from 10 (order does not matter).

2. A 4-digit PIN code, digits may repeat (n = 10 digits, p = 4).

3. A president and a secretary chosen from 10 members.

4. Putting 5 different books in a row on a shelf.

Reminder
order + all n → n! · order + p of n → A(n, p) · order + repeats → nᵖ · no order → C(n, p)
Show solution
  1. A team of 3 people chosen from 10 (order does not matter). C(n, p): a team is a group, order does not matter, nobody twice: combination.
  2. A 4-digit PIN code, digits may repeat (n = 10 digits, p = 4). nᵖ: ordered positions, a digit can come back: arrangement with repetition, 10⁴ = 10 000.
  3. A president and a secretary chosen from 10 members. A(n, p): the two jobs are different places, so order matters, nobody twice: arrangement A(10, 2) = 90.
  4. Putting 5 different books in a row on a shelf. n!: all 5 books are used, only the order counts: permutation 5! = 120.
A3

A podium

Warm-up

8 runners run a race. In how many ways can the gold, silver and bronze medals be given (no ties)?

Show solution

Order matters (gold ≠ silver), nobody gets two medals: arrangement without repetition.

A(8, 3) = 8 × 7 × 6 = 336.

A4

Letters of ANANAS

Standard

How many different words (with or without meaning) can be written with all 6 letters of the word ANANAS?

Show solution

All 6 letters are used, some are identical: permutation with repetition. Groups: A ×3, N ×2, S ×1.

6! / (3! × 2! × 1!) = 720 / 12 = 60.

A5

Two delegates

Warm-up

A class of 12 students elects 2 delegates with the same role. How many different pairs of delegates are possible?

Show solution

Same role, so {Ali, Sara} = {Sara, Ali}: order does not matter, combination.

C(12, 2) = 12 × 11 / 2 = 66.

A6

One of each colour

Standard

An urn holds 5 red and 3 green balls. You take 2 balls at once.

(a) How many ways give exactly 1 red and 1 green? (b) How many ways are there in total?

Show solution

(a) Choose 1 red among 5 AND 1 green among 3: C(5, 1) × C(3, 1) = 5 × 3 = 15.

(b) Any 2 of the 8 balls, taken at once (no order): C(8, 2) = 8 × 7 / 2 = 28.

So the chance of one of each colour would be 15/28.

A7

Same number

Standard

Which of these is equal to C(7, 2) = 21?

Show solution

Symmetry: C(n, p) = C(n, n − p), so C(7, 2) = C(7, 5) = 21.

A(7, 2) = 42 (order counted), 7 × 2 × 1 = 14, C(7, 3) = 35.

A8

A fruit basket

Challenge

A shop sells 4 kinds of fruit. You put 3 fruits in a basket; you may take the same kind several times and the order does not matter. How many different baskets?

Show solution

Combination with repetition, n = 4 kinds, p = 3 fruits: C(n + p − 1, p) = C(6, 3) = 20.

B

Events and probability rules (évènements et axiomes)

↑ contents
B1

Union on a die

Warm-up

Throw a die, Ω = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. A = “at least 5” and B = “even”. Which set is A ∪ B?

Show solution

A = {5, 6}, B = {2, 4, 6}. “A or B” keeps every face in at least one of them: A ∪ B = {2, 4, 5, 6}.

{6} is A ∩ B; {2, 4, 6} is only B.

B2

Using the rules

Standard

P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.2. Compute (a) P(A ∪ B), (b) P(Ā ∩ B̄), (c) P(A \ B).

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
P(Ā) = 1 − P(A)
P(A \ B) = P(A) − P(A ∩ B)
Show solution

(a) P(A ∪ B) = 0.5 + 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.7.

(b) De Morgan: Ā ∩ B̄ = not(A ∪ B), so 1 − 0.7 = 0.3.

(c) P(A \ B) = P(A) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.3.

B3

Spot the σ-algebra

Standard

Ω = {1, 2, 3}. Which family is a σ-algebra (tribu) on Ω?

Show solution

{∅, {3}, {1, 2}, Ω}: it contains Ω, each complement ({3} ↔ {1, 2}, ∅ ↔ Ω) and every union. It is {∅, A, Ā, Ω} with A = {3}.

{∅, {1}, {2}, {1, 2}, Ω}: the complement of {1} is {2, 3}, missing. {∅, {1}, {1, 2}}: Ω is missing. {{1}, {2, 3}, Ω}: ∅ (the complement of Ω) is missing.

B4

Two dice

Warm-up

Throw two fair dice (one red, one blue). What is the probability that the sum is 7?

Show solution

Ω = all pairs (red, blue): 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely outcomes.

Sum 7: (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1): 6 favourable. P = 6/36 = 1/6.

B5

Two white balls

Standard

An urn holds 6 white and 4 black balls. You take 2 at once. Probability that both are white?

Show solution

Possible: C(10, 2) = 45. Favourable: 2 of the 6 white, C(6, 2) = 15.

P = 15/45 = 1/3.

B6

Maths or physics

Standard

In a class of 30 students, 18 like maths, 12 like physics and 7 like both. (a) How many like at least one of the two? (b) A student is picked at random: probability he or she likes at least one?

Show solution

(a) Poincaré for 2 sets: 18 + 12 − 7 = 23 (the 7 were counted twice).

(b) Equally likely: 23/30 ≈ 0.767.

B7

Counting events

Warm-up

Ω = {a, b, c, d}. How many subsets (events) does the power set 𝒫(Ω) contain, counting ∅ and Ω?

Show solution

|Ω| = 4, so |𝒫(Ω)| = 2⁴ = 16. Each element is either in or out of a subset: 2 × 2 × 2 × 2.

C

Conditioning and independence (conditionnement et indépendance)

↑ contents
C1

A six, given even

Warm-up

Throw a fair die. Knowing that the result is even, what is the probability that it is a 6?

Show solution

B = even = {2, 4, 6}, P(B) = 1/2. A = {6}, A ∩ B = {6}, P = 1/6.

P(A | B) = (1/6) / (1/2) = 1/3: inside B there are 3 faces, one is a 6.

C2

Two white in a row

Standard

An urn holds 5 red and 3 white balls. Draw 2 balls one at a time, without putting back. Probability that both are white?

Show solution

1st white: 3/8. Then 2 white remain among 7: P(2nd white | 1st white) = 2/7.

Compound probability: 3/8 × 2/7 = 6/56 = 3/28 ≈ 0.107.

C3

Two machines

Standard

A factory has two machines. M1 makes 60% of the parts and M2 makes 40%. 2% of M1's parts and 5% of M2's parts are defective (D).

(a) A part is picked at random: probability it is defective? (b) It is defective: probability it came from M2?

P(D) = Σ P(D | Mᵢ) P(Mᵢ)
P(M₂ | D) = P(D | M₂) P(M₂) / P(D)
Show solution

(a) Total probability over {M1, M2}: P(D) = 0.6 × 0.02 + 0.4 × 0.05 = 0.012 + 0.020 = 0.032.

(b) Bayes: P(M2 | D) = 0.020 / 0.032 = 0.625. M2 makes fewer parts but most of the defective ones.

C4

Independent or not?

Standard

P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.2. What can you say about A and B?

Show solution

P(A) × P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 = P(A ∩ B): independent.

They are not incompatible, since P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 ≠ 0.

C5

Incompatible events

Standard

A and B are incompatible (A ∩ B = ∅), with P(A) > 0 and P(B) > 0. Are they independent?

Show solution

P(A ∩ B) = P(∅) = 0, but P(A) × P(B) > 0. The product rule fails: they are never independent. If A happens, B surely does not, so A carries information about B.

C6

Product rule

Challenge

A and B are independent, P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.6. Compute (a) P(A ∩ B) and (b) P(A ∪ B).

Show solution

(a) Independent: P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 × 0.6 = 0.18.

(b) P(A ∪ B) = 0.3 + 0.6 − 0.18 = 0.72.
